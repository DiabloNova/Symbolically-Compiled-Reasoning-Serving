# Research-to-publication workflow

## Governing objective

Transform the existing SCRS manuscript into a scientifically defensible, reproducible paper suitable for submission to a reputable peer-reviewed journal. Establish a maintained workflow that can regenerate the manuscript and its evidence artifacts as the work evolves.

A polished narrative is not a substitute for valid methods or evidence. Do not invent data, results, references, implementation behavior, or experimental outcomes. When evidence is missing, record the gap and choose explicitly between obtaining evidence, narrowing the claim, marking the claim as a hypothesis, or removing it.

## Stage 0 — Source integrity and inventory

- Recover a clean, readable copy of the canonical DOCX; preserve the supplied original and record SHA-256 hashes.
- Inventory all sections, equations, algorithms, tables, figures, references, appendices, and supplementary artifacts.
- Record each artifact's source, version, generation method, and whether it can be regenerated.
- Do not perform a full-document rewrite from a damaged or incomplete source copy.

**Exit gate:** canonical manuscript opens and can be compared with the supplied source; inventory is complete enough to trace all central claims.

## Stage 1 — Scientific audit before prose rewrite

Review, separately:
- research question, contribution, novelty, and positioning against related work;
- definitions, assumptions, scope, and notation consistency;
- mathematical derivations and boundary cases;
- algorithm specification, termination, counters, failure paths, and complexity;
- evaluation design, baselines, datasets, sample sizes, metrics, and statistical analysis;
- consistency between text, tables, figures, equations, and appendices;
- threats to validity, limitations, ethics/privacy, and reproducibility;
- citation coverage and whether references genuinely support each claim.

Classify each finding as **confirmed defect**, **unsupported claim**, **plausible concern**, or **resolved**. Do not silently replace source claims with assumptions.

**Exit gate:** a prioritized audit ledger is reviewed and each high-impact issue has a proposed evidence-backed disposition.

## Stage 2 — Claim-to-evidence ledger

Every central claim must map to one or more evidence items. Use these labels:
- **Source-derived:** explicitly present in the supplied paper.
- **Literature-supported:** supported by a verified external scholarly source.
- **Analytical:** follows from stated assumptions and a checkable derivation.
- **Measured:** produced by a documented experiment with provenance.
- **Simulated:** generated by a specified simulation.
- **Unresolved:** currently not adequately supported.

For measured results, record code/version, data source, configuration, environment, sample size, collection procedure, exclusions, uncertainty/statistical method, and artifact location. For simulated results, record the seed, distributions, parameter values, dependency versions, and exact command.

**Exit gate:** every central abstract/contribution claim has a traceable evidence path or is narrowed/removed.

## Stage 3 — Section-by-section scientific rewrite

Rewrite in dependency order, not merely from the first page onward:
1. problem statement and research question;
2. contributions and related work;
3. system model, definitions, and assumptions;
4. architecture and algorithm;
5. theoretical analysis;
6. experimental methodology;
7. results and statistical analysis;
8. discussion, threats to validity, limitations, and conclusion.

Keep a change log linking each rewritten section to the audit findings. Maintain consistent notation and cross-references. Do not claim journal suitability before evidence and validation gates pass.

## Stage 4 — Reproducible artifact pipeline

- Keep simulation/analysis code, configuration, environment/dependency lock, input data or documented access procedure, and output-generation scripts versioned.
- Generate tables and figures from machine-readable results, not manual edits.
- Include automated checks for equations' numerical examples, probability sums, schema validity, table values, and figure/source consistency where practical.
- Record output hashes and the code commit used for every manuscript release.
- Separate synthetic scenario artifacts from empirical experiments in directory names, filenames, figure captions, and prose.

**Exit gate:** a clean checkout can regenerate the declared artifacts using documented commands, and output differences are explained.

## Stage 5 — Independent verification

A reviewer who did not author the analysis should verify:
- central claims against evidence;
- math and algorithm invariants;
- table/figure values against generated source data;
- reproducibility from a clean environment;
- appropriate uncertainty, baselines, and limitations;
- citations and bibliographic metadata;
- absence of unsupported empirical language.

## Stage 6 — Journal-specific submission preparation

Only after the scientific core is stable:
- select candidate journals based on scope, contribution type, standards, and recent comparable papers;
- verify current author guidelines from the journal itself;
- adapt abstract, structure, word count, reference style, figures, supplementary materials, data/code statements, and ethics declarations;
- prepare a cover letter and a transparent limitations statement.

No journal acceptance or publication readiness can be guaranteed by editing alone.

## Decision rule

When a claim cannot be supported, do not disguise the gap with stronger prose. Prefer, in order: obtain the missing evidence; derive a valid result from declared assumptions; narrow the claim; label it as a hypothesis or scenario; or remove it.
